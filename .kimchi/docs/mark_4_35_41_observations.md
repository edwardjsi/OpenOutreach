# 50 Observations on Mark 4:35-41

*A Grace/Pentecostal Exegetical Study*

---

## CONTEXT & SETTING (1-5)

**1. Evening Transition**
"Evening came" (ὀψίας γενομένης) — Mark's Gospel moves from a full day of teaching (the parables chapter) to this overnight crossing. Jesus initiates the crossing, not the disciples. *The Shepherd leads the sheep into the storm.*

**2. "Let Us Go Over to the Other Side"**
Jesus initiates the journey. He is not fleeing the crowd — He is advancing toward something. The "other side" was Gentile territory (Decapolis). There was a demon-possessed man waiting (Mark 5:1). The storm was not a detour; it was part of the journey.

**3. "Just As He Was"**
No special provisions packed. No luggage. Jesus crossed with the barest minimum. This tells us He traveled lightly and trusted the Father for every need — a pattern for Kingdom advance.

**4. Another Boat**
Mark 4:36 mentions "other boats" accompanying them. This detail is unique to Mark. There were witnesses to the miracle, not just the Twelve. The storm and calm happened before an audience.

**5. The Sea of Galilee Geography**
This lake is 13 miles long, 8 miles wide, and 140 feet deep at its lowest point. Sudden violent storms are meteorologically common when cold air from the Hermon mountains hits the warm water. Jesus was not asleep in a gentle drizzle — this was a legitimately dangerous squall.

## THE STORM & THE DISCIPLES (6-15)

**6. "A Terrible Squall Developed"**
The Greek: "λαίλαψ ἀνέμου μεγάλη" — a hurricane-force windstorm. The word *lailaps* (λαίλαψ) appears only here in the New Testament. Mark, who more than likely was Peter's interpreter and wrote from memory, chooses a rare, vivid word for impact.

**7. "Waves Beat Into the Boat"**
Not merely choppy water — the boat was taking on water and filling. The present tense of the verbs suggests continuous action: the waves KEPT beating, the boat KEPT filling. This was life-threatening, ongoing danger.

**8. "So That the Boat Was Already Filling"**
The Greek (ἤδη ἐγεμίζετο) literally: "was already being filled." The disaster was in progress. They were not imagining danger — it was objectively happening.

**9. "He Was in the Stern, Sleeping on a Cushion"**
The word for "sleeping" (ἐκάθευδεν) describes deep, sound sleep — not a light nap. The cushion (προσκεφάλαιον) was likely a leather or cloth headrest. Jesus slept like someone who had absolutely no anxiety about the journey.

**10. The Disciples Wake Him — With Urgency**
They "woke him" (διεγείρουσιν αὐτόν) — the prefix διά suggests thorough, complete waking. They shook Him. This was not a gentle tap. They were desperate.

**11. "Teacher, Do You Not Care That We Are Perishing?"**
Three reveals in this question:
- *Teacher* (διδάσκαλε) — they acknowledged His authority but only as a Rabbi, not as Lord of the storm
- *Do you not care?* (οὐ μέλει σοι;) — accusation against His character; they thought His love had limits
- *We are perishing* (ἀπολλύμεθα) — the same verb used for the lost (cf. Mark 1:17, "fishers of men") — they believed death was imminent

**12. This Is the Cry of the Damned**
"ἀπολλύμεθα" is the present middle voice — "we are destroying ourselves" or "we are being destroyed." It is the language of those who believe no rescue is coming. The disciples, in a moment of terror, spoke faithlessness out loud.

**13. The Disciples Had Previously Witnessed His Teaching Authority**
They had just heard Mark 4:9: "Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear." They heard Him teach with authority that astonished them (Mark 1:22). Yet authority over nature did not automatically transfer to their trust in a crisis.

**14. The Storms Reveal What Is Already In Us**
Luke 8:14 adds that the seed that fell among thorns "choked" by "the worries and riches and pleasures of life." The storm was an X-ray of pre-existing unbelief planted in the heart. External trials often expose internal theological gaps.

**15. They Had Multiple Reasons to Trust Him**
They had seen Him:
- Heal the sick (Mark 1:34)
- Cast out demons (Mark 1:34)
- Teach with authority (Mark 1:22)
- Feed thousands with five loaves (Mark 6:44 — this comes AFTER this event, but Mark writes chronologically out of order in his Gospel)

Yet none of this was enough. Fear is not defeated by past miracles — it is defeated by present trust.

## THE CALMING (16-25)

**16. "He Got Up"**
From sleep to action in one movement. He did not argue with the storm. He did not pray for intervention. He stood and spoke. *Authority looks like this — one word and creation obeys.*

**17. "He Rebuked the Wind"**
The word for "rebuked" (ἐπετίμησεν) is the same word used for rebuking demons (Mark 1:25, 9:25). Jesus treats the wind as a personal, hostile force — not merely weather, but spiritual opposition. This is cosmic warfare language.

**18. "Quiet! Be Still!"**
"Σιώπα! Πεφίμωσο!" — These are imperatives of absolute authority. Σιώπα means "silence" (to stop making noise). Πεφίμωσο means "be muzzled" (as one muzzles an animal). Jesus addresses the sea as if it were a disorderly beast. There is almost humorous restraint in creation when He speaks — it obeys instantly.

**19. The Effect Was Immediate and Total**
"ἐκόπασεν ὁ ἄνεμος" — "the wind ceased." No tailing off, no gradual lessening. Full stop. The Greek aorist tense indicates completed action at a point. It is done.

**20. "There Was a Great Calm"**
"γαλήνη ἐγένετο μεγάλη" — A great calm became. The same word *egeneto* used of creation ("and it was so"). Jesus speaks and reality reshapes itself by the power of His word.

**21. "The Greek Word for "Calm" (Galene)"**
Interestingly, Galene (γαλήνη) was also the name of a Greek goddess of the calm sea. Mark may be subtly contrasting the true God over against mythological deities. The real calm versus the imaginary gods.

**22. Jesus Spoke to the Storm — Not About It**
He did not pray, "Father, calm this storm." He addressed it directly with a Word from God. This is prophetic ministry — speaking to the problem as if it has ears. This is the model for spiritual warfare.

**23. Nature Obeyed Before the Disciples Understood**
The wind stopped while Jesus was still speaking. The sentence "He said to the sea..." and "the wind ceased" are almost simultaneous in Greek structure. Faith does not wait for understanding before God moves. He moves, and then we catch up.

**24. The Miraculous as Sign**
Jesus did not explain this miracle away or tell them to keep it quiet. He acted, and the result was revelation. Mark records this as a Christological sign — the Kingdom had arrived in the person of Jesus, and even the elements submitted to Him.

**25. The Disciples Had No Part in the Miracle**
They did not pray, fast, or exercise faith in that moment. The miracle was pure grace. Jesus acted while they panicked. Grace does not wait for your faith level to be high enough — it acts in your helplessness.

## THE REVELATION EXCHANGE (26-35)

**26. "He Turned to His Disciples"**
Mark 4:36 shifts from the cosmic drama back to the intimate. After calming the storm, Jesus turns His attention to the learners. The miracle was not primarily for the sea — it was for them.

**27. "Why Are You So Afraid?"**
Jesus diagnoses the REAL problem. The storm was external. Fear was internal. The wind was not their enemy — their lack of faith was. This is a word to every believer: your storm is not the diagnosis; your reaction to it is.

**28. "So Afraid" — The Intensity**
The Greek "δειλοὶ" (deiloi) means cowardly, timid. This is the word for someone who is paralyzed by fear. And it comes from the same root as "δείλη" meaning "evening" — twilight, when the sun disappears and darkness comes. Fear is living in perpetual twilight.

**29. "Have You Still No Faith?"**
The word "still" (ἔτι) is devastating. It implies they OUGHT to have developed faith by now. After everything they had witnessed, they should have grown. But they had not. Jesus is noting a spiritual stagnation.

**30. "No Faith" — Not "Little Faith"**
Matthew 8:26 says "little faith" (ὀλιγοπιστίαν). Mark says NO faith (ἄπιστοι — "unbelieving"). The distinction may indicate Mark is recording a harsher exchange, or that Jesus is making a stronger point: the gap between them and belief was not small — it was a chasm.

**31. "How Is It You Have No Faith?" (Mark 4:40 — literal translation)**
The Greek "πῶς οὐκ ἔχετε πίστιν;" is a question of astonishment. Jesus is amazed at their unbelief. This is the same Jesus who "marveled" at the centurion's faith in Matthew 8:10. He is consistently surprised by BOTH extremes — by great faith AND by the absence of it.

**32. "They Feared a Great Fear"**
Mark 4:41 — "ἐφοβήθησαν φόβον μέγαν." This is a Hebraism — a fearful fear, an intensification. They were terrified not just by the storm, but by the MAN who had just commanded it into silence. The magnitude of the miracle now hit them.

**33. "Who Is This?"**
"For they were terrified and asked one another, 'Who is this? Even the wind and the waves obey him!'" This is the question EVERY storm is designed to answer. Jesus does not explain who He is. He lets the storm do it. When God speaks to your chaos and it obeys, you learn who He is.

**34. The Revelation Principle**
God does not give a theological lecture on His deity. He acts, and understanding follows. Discipleship through experience is Mark's pattern. They learned who Jesus was BY what He did — not by what He explained.

**35. The Disciples Grew in Understanding Slowly**
Mark notes their fear, not their worship. They did not fall at His feet in adoration (cf. Matthew 14:33 — a later post-Resurrection event where they DID worship). This was a first step. Revelation is progressive.

## GREEK WORD STUDIES (36-42)

**36. The Word for "Rebuked" — Epitimao (ἐπετίμησεν)**
This word carries the idea of ENFORCING authority. To rebuke is to exercise functional authority over something that needs to submit. Jesus was not requesting — He was decreeing. This word appears in:
- Mark 1:25 — rebuking an unclean spirit
- Mark 3:12 — rebuking demons not to make Him known
- Mark 9:25 — rebuking the deaf-mute spirit

The wind was operating as a demonized force. This was not random weather.

**37. The Word for "Be Still" — Pephimoso (πεφίμωσο)**
A perfect passive imperative of φιμόω — to muzzle, to silence, to put a bridle on. Jesus is treating the sea like a wild animal. The passive voice means: "Let yourself be silenced." The sea was given an imperative to submit to the voice that created it (Genesis 1:9-10 — "let the waters be gathered").

**38. The Word for "Fear" — Phobos (φόβος)**
Fear in the New Testament is often the antithesis of faith. Romans 8:15: "For you did not receive a spirit of slavery that leads you again to fear." The disciples had the Spirit of adoption (theology of Grace) but were living in the Spirit of fear. This is a failure to walk in what they had already been given.

**39. The Word for "Faith" — Pistis (πίστις)**
In the New Testament, faith is not mere mental assent — it is active trust that produces obedience. James 2:17: "Faith by itself, if it does not have works, is dead." The disciples' "faith" was inert. It existed as doctrine but had no functional power in the storm.

**40. The Word for "Perishing" — Apollymetha (ἀπολλύμεθα)**
This is the present middle/passive indicative — "we are being destroyed" or "we are perishing." It is related to "apollumi" — to destroy, to lose. The same word used for the lost (Luke 15:17 — the prodigal "came to his senses" after being "lost"). The disciples believed they were being lost. Jesus had already bought them with His blood — they were not. This was a lie from the pit of hell.

**41. The Word for "Still" in "He Was Still Asleep"**
The Greek: "αὐτὸς ἦν καθεύδων" — the imperfect tense, ongoing action in past time. He was in the process of sleeping soundly. The word "still" is interpretive — the Greek actually emphasizes His SLEEP, not the continuation. He was simply asleep, deeply and peacefully.

**42. The Word for "Cushion" — Proskephalaion (προσκεφάλαιον)**
Literally "something placed before the head." A headrest. Jesus was not in the bow (front) of the boat where He would be exposed — He was in the stern (back), the most sheltered place. He slept in the place of security while the bow was being battered. This is a picture of abiding in the place of rest even when circumstances are chaotic.

## THEOLOGICAL THEMES (43-46)

**43. Christology — Jesus Is Lord Over Creation**
This is an early high Christology moment in Mark. Jesus does not petition God to calm the storm — He commands creation as if He has inherent authority over it. This aligns with John 1:3: "All things were made through Him." The sea was His.

**44. Pneumatology — The Wind/Spirit Connection**
In Hebrew, "Ruach" means both wind and Spirit. In Mark 4:26-29 (the parable of the growing seed), the Spirit is at work mysteriously in growth. In Mark 4:35-41, the Spirit's domain (wind/Ruach) is being rebuked by Jesus. The Spirit of God submits to the Son of God.

**45. Soteriology — The Storm as Picture of the Christian Life**
The boat = the Church. The storm = trials. Jesus is present even when He seems inactive. The miracle is not that the storm came — it is that they arrived on the other side. Salvation is not the absence of storms but safe passage THROUGH them.

**46. Eschatology — "The Other Side"**
The other side was demon territory (the Gadarene demoniac, Mark 5:1). Jesus was advancing into enemy-occupied territory. The Church crosses through troubled waters to reach the mission field. The "other side" is where the lost are waiting.

## HOMILETICAL IMPLICATIONS (47-50)

**47. Preaching the Text, Not Impressing the Text**
Mark leaves us with a question, not an explanation. "Who is this?" The Gospel does not answer — it presents Jesus and invites response. Good preaching does not explain away mystery but leads people to encounter the Person.

**48. The Problem Is Never the Problem**
The devil wants you to focus on the storm — the job, the diagnosis, the relationship. Jesus redirects to faith — the issue is BELIEVING who you say you believe. The storm is an occasion for faith to manifest, not an obstacle to faith.

**49. Grace Comes While You Panic**
The miracle happened while they were in panic. Grace did not wait for them to calm down, straighten out their theodicy, or resolve their doubt. Grace acted in their chaos. Romans 5:6: "While we were still sinners, Christ died for us." Salvation is not the reward for getting your faith together — it is the intervention while you are falling apart.

**50. The Goal Is Worship, Not Just Peace**
Mark 4:41 ends with a question: "Who is this?" The goal of every storm is a more profound revelation of Jesus. If you go through a trial and only get peace, you have missed something. The trial was designed to answer the question: who is He to YOU? The correct answer is always: "Even the wind and waves obey Him — therefore I can trust Him with my storm."
